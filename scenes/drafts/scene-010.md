# Scene 010: Drop The Hammer
**Status:** Draft
**Themes:** fatherhood, presence, the small moments, time, surrender, love
**Connects to:** Scene 007, Scene 009

---

## Monologue

10:16 PM.

The case can wait.

Across the table — or the couch, or the floor, wherever the arena materialized — sits the opponent. Small. Strung together with something between wire and lightning. Four years of life on this earth, almost five, which in his mind is practically a decade of experience. He sets his elbow with the seriousness of someone who has studied the sport.

The detective considers his options.

He could win. Technically. Physically. The math is not close. But math has never been the point of anything that matters and he knows this by now, has known it for a while, paid for the knowledge in ways he doesn't talk about at gas stations.

They lock hands.

The grip is fierce. Disproportionately fierce.

He thinks about letting him win. Plots the angle of surrender. How to make it look earned. How to lose in a way that feels like victory to a four year old who is almost five who has been waiting all day probably for this exact moment.

And then.

The little string one drops the hammer.

Fast. Certain. No mercy.

The detective's hand hits the surface.

He didn't have to let him win.

He just lost.

The victor does not gloat quietly. The victor announces. The victor may or may not do a thing with his arms that should be documented. The detective accepts the verdict with the grace of a man who has learned that some defeats are the best thing that ever happened to you.

10:16 PM.

The drive home — somewhere between the wave and the billboard and the rattling cupholder and all those stars — either happened or didn't. He can't place it now. The details blurred out the moment a four year old with almost five years experience grabbed his hand and meant it.

Some rides home don't end at the door.

Some end here.

Right here.
