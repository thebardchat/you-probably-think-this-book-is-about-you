# Scene 007: The Loop
**Status:** Draft
**Themes:** routine, time, the cycle, identity, momentum, the body
**Connects to:** Scene 002, Scene 003, Scene 004

---

## Monologue

The front door takes the hit.

Not violent. Just certain. The kind of entry that announces itself without meaning to — bull through the matador, less snot, less theater, no red cape required. Shoes leave the feet before the thought forms. Clothes follow. The house receives him the way houses do — without ceremony, without complaint.

It hums.

Games playing somewhere. Television in another room doing what televisions do — talking to a wall, filling silence with noise that passes for company. The dog — the veteran — makes his assessment. Surveys the returning figure. Decides the spoils are acceptable. Claims his post at the foot of the bed.

Bed.

Boom.

Alarm.

Three S's executed with military efficiency. Shower. Something. Something. The body knowing the sequence before the brain catches up. Muscle memory. The gospel of the daily.

Hit the door the same way he arrived.

Different direction.

The night sky stops him for exactly three seconds.

Stars — more than the suburbs usually allow — crowding the dark like they've been waiting. Can he count them. Not now. But he saw them. He *saw* them. That's the part that matters. That's the part that doesn't make it into any report.

Crank the truck.

The drive opens up ahead of him like it was never closed. Gas station. Fluorescent cathedral. Cold can. The old man materializes — same proximity, same yesterday smell, same harmless orbit. Vape blooms in the parking lot air and dissolves into whatever dissolves into.

He stands at the register.

Was this last night.

Is this today.

The receipt prints either way.

Clock in.

The case file opens on the desk exactly where he left it — or maybe it's a new one that looks identical. The detective doesn't check. Doesn't need to.

Work mode.

The film keeps rolling.

## Notes
Scene 007. The full loop. Day becomes night becomes day.
Callbacks to scenes 002, 003, 004 — the reader recognizes them now. Familiarity is the point.
Time collapses. The mundane as both trap and rhythm.
"He saw them" — the stars — is the heartbeat buried in the routine. Easy to miss. Intentional.
