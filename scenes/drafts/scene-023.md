# Scene 023: The Teenagers' File
**Status:** Draft
**Themes:** youth, invincibility, the unformed, becoming, the vape, borrowed time
**Connects to:** Scene 004, Scene 018, Scene 022

---

## Monologue

They don't have a file yet.

That's the first thing the doctor says. Sets the folder on the desk and it is thin — not empty, but thin, the way files are thin when the subject hasn't finished happening yet. Most of the pages are blank. Not waiting to be filled. Just — not yet. The ink still deciding what it is.

The doctor opens it anyway.

---

Here is what we have.

Everything and nothing simultaneously. The particular condition of being seventeen in a gas station at an hour that requires explanation to adults and requires nothing to themselves. They are here because here is where they are. The logic is complete. The logic has always been complete at seventeen and the tragedy and the beauty of seventeen is that this is actually true — the logic IS complete, for seventeen. It just doesn't transfer.

They don't know that yet.

The doctor notes: they are not supposed to know that yet. Knowing it too early costs something that cannot be replaced. The not-knowing is load-bearing.

---

There is a confidence in this file.

Disproportionate, structurally unsound, magnificent. The kind of confidence that hasn't been load-tested yet — not because it is false but because the loads haven't arrived in their full weight. They will. They always do. Tuesday at thirty-four will deliver something that seventeen could not have calculated for, and the confidence will crack in the specific place it was always going to crack, and then the interesting part begins.

But not yet.

Right now the confidence walks to the counter and negotiates with the fluency of someone who has never been told no in a way that rearranged them. The cashier processes the transaction. The doctor processes the confidence. Files it under: temporary, irreplaceable, handle with care.

The doctor notes: the detective was this once. Has forgotten in the specific way adults forget — not completely, just enough to find it foreign.

---

There is a vape in this file.

Small. Flavored. Something tropical that never grew in any ground. The doctor examines it the way you examine a symbol that doesn't know it's a symbol. The teenagers don't see it as a statement. Don't see it as rebellion or ritual or the specific human need to hold something and exhale something and feel briefly that the interior weather has been addressed.

They just like it.

Which is its own kind of statement.

The doctor notes: every generation finds its thing. The thing is never the point. The thing is the community around the thing. The shared language of it. The password. The doctor has seen enough files to know that the thing changes and the need for the thing doesn't.

It dissolves in the wind.

Everything they're certain about right now dissolves in the wind eventually.

The doctor says this without judgment. Judgment is for people who have forgotten that their certainties dissolved too.

---

There is something in this file about being seen.

Or specifically about not being seen. About moving through spaces where adults look past you or look at you with the particular exhaustion of people who have forgotten what it costs to be unfinished. The detective clocked them as a scene. The cashier processed them as a transaction. The old man didn't clock them at all — too close to his own interior weather to notice theirs.

Nobody asked them what they were building.

Nobody asked because nobody assumed they were building anything.

But they are.

Badly, beautifully, without blueprints, without permits, without any guarantee that what goes up will stand. They are building the thing that will one day be a person who drives to a gas station at 5am for a cold can and notices a teenager at the counter and files them away as a scene.

The circle.

The doctor marks it in the margin.

---

There is a future in this file that the file doesn't show yet.

Blank pages. The doctor fans through them slowly. One of these teenagers becomes someone who locks the file down — good life, steady, the garbage at the curb on Tuesday, the body voting yes every morning, the house that becomes his through maintenance and love. One becomes someone with a wound that gets a date and a name and becomes a scar that gets touched sometimes like a sensitive tooth. One becomes someone who builds things — chains of things, each connected to the next, extending into a distance they absolutely intend to reach.

One becomes the detective.

The doctor doesn't say which one.

Doesn't matter which one.

Matters that the file stays open.

---

The vape cloud catches the wind outside.

Tropical. Thin. Gone.

The doctor watches it go.

Notes in the margin: they are not behind. They are exactly where the file requires them to be. The blank pages are not absence. The blank pages are the whole point.

You can't write the ending before you've lived the middle.

Nobody told them that yet.

Lucky them.
