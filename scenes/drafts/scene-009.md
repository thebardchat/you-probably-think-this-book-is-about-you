# Scene 009: The Wave
**Status:** Draft
**Themes:** connection, expectation, the unspoken, strangers, separate paths
**Connects to:** Scene 007, Scene 002

---

## Monologue

Cruise control.

The truck decides its own speed and the detective lets it. Surrenders the foot. Becomes a passenger in his own vehicle, which is either deeply philosophical or just highway driving depending on how awake you are.

Music or news.

Neither.

The silence wins without a fight tonight. Not the heavy kind — not the silence that needs filling. The good kind. The kind that has texture. The rattle from the passenger door joins in, something shifting in the cupholder, a roll of something or a forgotten something, not enough to matter, just enough to remind him the truck is alive in its own way. He lets it rattle. It's company without obligation.

Outside, the stars are working overtime.

Moon punching above its weight. The road ahead lit by something older than electricity — that silver prehistoric glow that guided people long before anyone thought to pave anything. He drives inside it. The early hour earns this. Leave late, fight everyone. Leave early, inherit the road. The math is simple. The reward is real.

No rush.

He slows.

A car wants over. He makes space — unhurried, unannounced. Just moves. And then.

The question forms before he can stop it.

*Is he going to wave.*

Not said out loud. Just thought. That small involuntary audit of the social contract — will this stranger, granted safe passage through the dark by a man he'll never meet, acknowledge the gift. A hand. A flash of lights. Anything.

Or does the detective wave first. Without looking. The preemptive grace. The wave that expects nothing and offers everything.

He can't remember which one he did.

Doesn't matter.

Two cars. Two separate paths. One moment where they were briefly, improbably, considerate of each other.

Then gone.

The dark takes them both.

The road doesn't record any of it.
