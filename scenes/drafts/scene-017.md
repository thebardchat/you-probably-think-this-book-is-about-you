# Scene 017: The Inventory
**Status:** Draft
**Themes:** memory, the universal, loss, the mundane, accumulation, the irretrievable
**Connects to:** Scene 016

---

## Monologue

It goes further than pockets.

There's the stove you didn't turn off except you did but you'll wonder about it until the driveway. The door you locked but the handle will get checked three times at the airport because the brain doesn't trust itself on the things that matter. The iron definitely off but maybe not. The dog with water yes but how much water, was it enough water, he looked fine but dogs don't complain until they do. The oven that has been off for eleven years of your adult life except for the one time you left a frozen pizza box inside it and the house smelled like poor decisions for a week.

There's the email you sent and immediately needed back — the one that went to the wrong person or the right person at the wrong time or the right person at the right time with the wrong word in the third paragraph that changes the entire meaning of the sentence in a way you didn't catch until the send button was already in the past. The text you misread as cold and responded to with warmth and it was fine and the text you misread as warm and responded to with brevity and it was not fine and you found out later in a different conversation entirely.

There's the thing you said at the dinner table in 1997 that you remember and nobody else does or everyone else does and nobody mentions it. The joke that landed in a room of silence so complete you could hear your own confidence leaving through the nearest exit. The apology you workshopped in your head for three days and delivered in fourteen seconds and drove away before the other person could respond so you'll never actually know if it landed. The argument you won that you still feel like you lost. The argument you lost that you were right about.

There's the form you were supposed to return. The bill that is probably fine but has been probably fine for long enough that it has become an ambient hum in the back of the administrative brain where all the probably fines live together in a city that never sleeps. The appointment rescheduled twice that will get rescheduled a third time unless something changes which it won't. The book half read on the nightstand that watches you sleep with the quiet judgment of an unfinished thing. The gym you joined and then visited seven times and then began referring to as something you do in the sense that it is somewhere you have been.

The voicemail from someone you love that you saved and then lost in a phone upgrade and can't get back and know the words of but cannot hear the voice of anymore not exactly not the real version and you've tried to reconstruct it from memory but memory is an unreliable narrator as established.

The photograph not taken at the moment you knew you should take it because your hands were full or you didn't want to seem like someone who takes photographs at moments like this and now the moment is a painting in the mind that gets slightly less detailed every year and you maintain it the way you maintain anything you can't afford to lose — carefully, quietly, hoping nobody asks you to prove it's still there.

The friend you meant to call back on a Tuesday that became Wednesday that became a season that became a different kind of distance that has no name because naming it would require admitting it. The birthday you remembered at 11:47 PM and sent a message anyway and they said no worries but you both knew. The compliment you thought and never said because the moment passed in the time it took to decide whether saying it would be strange and now the person carries the day without knowing what you thought of them.

The version of yourself you were in a particular year that you cannot locate now no matter which drawer you check. The confidence you had before the thing that happened. The lightness before the weight arrived and made itself at home. The specific way you laughed before you learned to monitor your laugh. The dream you had every night for a year and then never again and can't fully remember except the feeling of it which visits sometimes without the pictures.

The thing you were going to do differently.

The thing you were going to do differently.

The thing you were going to do differently.

Some people go back for all of it. Spend the whole drive turned around looking for what they left behind, missing every exit that was meant for them, arriving late to their own life.

Some people leave it.

Not because it didn't matter.

Because the road ahead doesn't wait and the thing — whatever the thing was — is either still there or it isn't and you are still here and the morning is still happening and that is not nothing.

That is not nothing at all.
