# Scene 004: Body Fuel
**Status:** Draft
**Themes:** addiction, generation, routine, cost of living, irony, the body
**Connects to:** Scene 003

---

## Monologue

Pull over.

The body needs what the body needs.

Gas station. Fluorescent lights doing their best impression of daylight. The kind of place that exists in every city and belongs to none of them — same coolers, same spin rack of sunglasses nobody buys, same floor that's been mopped into submission for thirty years.

The detective grabs the cold can. The familiar one. The one that promises energy it may or may not deliver.

Behind him, teenagers negotiate the counter with the confidence of people who have never been told no in a way that stuck. Vapes. Flavored. Small enough to hide, profitable enough to stock. The cashier processes the transaction without processing it. Nobody is the villain here. Nobody is innocent either.

The old man materializes from nowhere.

Standing close. Too close. The kind of close that skips the social contract entirely — not rude, just elsewhere. Somewhere back in a decade when personal space was still being invented. He smells like yesterday and means no harm.

Fourteen dollars and change.

Credit card. The small surrender. The detective signs nothing, confirms nothing, just taps the future against a reader and walks out.

Outside, the wind has an opinion.

The vape cloud from the teenagers catches it — artificial watermelon or something tropical, something that never grew in any ground — and it stretches thin across the parking lot and disappears into the dark like it was never a thing at all.

The can cracks open.

Cold. Carbonated. Exactly what was needed.

The body doesn't ask why. Just says thank you and moves on.
