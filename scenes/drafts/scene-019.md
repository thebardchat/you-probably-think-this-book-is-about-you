# Scene 019: Not A Bill
**Status:** Draft
**Themes:** cost, answers, the examined life, irony, the unbilled, worth
**Connects to:** Scene 018

---

## Monologue

The envelope arrives the way they always do.

Uninvited. Certain. Wearing the specific font of institutions that have decided professionalism is a color scheme. He turns it over. There it is — that particular kindness of the medical industrial complex, that small mercy printed in bold so you see it before you see anything else:

*This Is Not A Bill.*

He stares at it.

For what.

The doctor who used no instruments. Left no prescription. Drew no blood. Ordered no imaging of the thing that needed imaging most — that three pound electrical storm behind the eyes that has been running its own diagnostics since before anyone thought to ask it questions. No co-pay at the door. No intake form asking him to rate his pain on a scale that has never had enough numbers.

Just a room.

Just eyes that stayed interested instead of surprised.

Just the account read back to him in language he recognized but hadn't assembled himself yet.

*This Is Not A Bill.*

Then what is it.

He reads further. The itemized nothing. The explanation of benefits that explains without clarifying. The balance due of a visit he can't quite locate in any calendar but feels in the specific way you feel things that actually happened. Somewhere between the arm wrestling match and the drive and the stars he sat in that room and got taken apart with the lights on and put back together in the same order and somehow that order made more sense than it did before.

For what.

For nothing that fits a billing code.

For the answers he already had but couldn't find because he was looking in the wrong rooms. For the questions he wanted answered and the ones he didn't know he was asking and the ones the doctor declined to answer because some questions are not the doctor's to answer — those belong to the man, to the drive, to the 10:16 PM and the stars too numerous to count but not too numerous to see.

*This Is Not A Bill.*

Maybe that's the whole thing right there.

The most important visits cost nothing you can pay back. The most important examinations leave no record. The most useful answers are the ones you already knew and needed someone to read back to you slowly in a quiet room while you pretended you didn't know.

He folds the envelope.

Puts it in the cupholder.

Next to the rattle.

Next to everything else riding along that has no name and takes up no space and costs exactly everything.
